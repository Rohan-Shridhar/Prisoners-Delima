### Descriptions of Tournament Strategies

**1. One man (Cooperate):**
This is the most straightforward and optimistic strategy. It always chooses to cooperate in every single round, no matter what the
opponent does. It is a purely "nice" strategy that never initiates a defection, hoping for mutual cooperation.

**2. Zero man (Defect):**
The polar opposite of "One man," this strategy is purely antagonistic. It defects in every single round from the very beginning,
regardless of the opponent's actions. It aims to maximize its own score by exploiting cooperative players, even at the cost of
mutual punishment against other defectors.

**3. Tit-for-Tat:**
A famous and historically successful strategy, Tit-for-Tat is both forgiving and retaliatory. It begins by cooperating in the first
round. For every subsequent round, it simply copies the opponent's move from the previous round. If the opponent cooperates, it
cooperates; if the opponent defects, it defects in return.

**4. Punisher (Grim Trigger):**
This strategy is cooperative but has an unforgiving memory. It starts by cooperating and will continue to do so as long as the
opponent cooperates. However, the very first time the opponent defects, this strategy will "trigger" and defect for the rest of the
entire match, never cooperating again.

**5. Tit-for-Two-Tats:**
A more forgiving version of Tit-for-Tat, this strategy gives the opponent a second chance. It will only defect if the opponent has
defected in the last two consecutive rounds. A single defection from the opponent is tolerated, but two in a row will provoke a
defection in response.

**6. Tit-for-Three-Tats:**
This is an even more lenient strategy than Tit-for-Two-Tats. It patiently cooperates until the opponent defects for three
consecutive rounds. Only after a pattern of three straight defections will it retaliate by defecting.

**7. Two Tit for Tat:**
This strategy is a variation of Tit-for-Tat that punishes a defection more severely. When an opponent defects, this strategy will
retaliate by defecting for the next two rounds. It starts by cooperating and only defects as a two-round punishment for each of the
opponent's defections.

**8. Johan:**
This strategy acts as a direct counter to a simple Tit-for-Tat player. It starts by cooperating and then, for every subsequent
round, it does the opposite of what the opponent did in the previous round. If the opponent cooperated, it defects; if the opponent
defected, it cooperates.

**9. One for Even Man:**
This is a simple, predictable, and non-adaptive strategy. It defects on odd-numbered rounds (1, 3, 5, etc.) and cooperates on
even-numbered rounds (2, 4, 6, etc.). Its moves are based solely on the round number, not on the opponent's behavior.

**10. Zero for Even Man:**
The inverse of "One for Even Man," this strategy also follows a predictable, non-adaptive pattern. It cooperates on odd-numbered
rounds (1, 3, 5, etc.) and defects on even-numbered rounds (2, 4, 6, etc.), ignoring the opponent's actions entirely.

**11. Player 1100:**
This is a pattern-based strategy that is not reactive to its opponent. It follows a repeating four-round cycle: Cooperate,
Cooperate, Defect, Defect. This pattern continues indefinitely throughout the match.

**12. Player 0011:**
The reverse of Player 1100, this strategy also follows a fixed, repeating four-round cycle. Its pattern is: Defect, Defect,
Cooperate, Cooperate. It does not adapt its behavior based on the opponent's moves.

**13. Player 0111:**
This is another pattern-based strategy that repeats a four-round sequence. It begins with a defection and then cooperates for the
next three rounds (Defect, Cooperate, Cooperate, Cooperate). This cycle repeats regardless of the opponent's actions.

**14. Player 1000:**
The inverse of Player 0111, this strategy follows a fixed four-round pattern of one cooperation followed by three defections
(Cooperate, Defect, Defect, Defect). It is not a reactive strategy and sticks to its sequence.

**15. Joss (Win-Stay, Lose-Shift):**
Also known as Pavlov, this strategy bases its decisions on its own success. It starts by cooperating. If it "wins" a round (earning
a high payoff of 3 or 5 points), it will repeat its last move. If it "loses" (earning a low payoff of 0 or 1 point), it will switch
its move for the next round.

**16. Columbus:**
This strategy is a variation of the "Punisher" or "Grim Trigger" concept. It cooperates by default, but if it detects that the
opponent has defected for two consecutive rounds, it will switch to defecting for the remainder of the match.

**17. Galileo:**
Similar to Columbus but more tolerant, this strategy also cooperates by default. It will only switch to a permanent state of
defection if the opponent defects for three consecutive rounds. It is more forgiving of isolated or paired defections.

**18. Sharp:**
This strategy makes a critical judgment based on the very first interaction. It starts by cooperating. If the opponent defects in the
first round, "Sharp" will defect for every single subsequent round. If the opponent cooperates in the first round, "Sharp" will
cooperate for the rest of the match.

**19. Rayon:**
This is a complex, multi-phase strategy. It cooperates for the first 10 rounds, then defects on round 11. Afterwards, it enters a
reactive phase where it defects if the opponent has defected twice in a row, defects if its own score is lower, and otherwise plays
a mix of retaliatory and forgiving moves based on recent history.

**20. Sigma:**
This strategy has an initial cooperative phase, cooperating for the first three rounds. After that, its logic is simple: it checks
the scores. If the opponent's score is higher than its own, it will defect. Otherwise, it will cooperate.

**21. Sure:**
This strategy begins by cooperating for the first two rounds to test the waters. After that, it checks if the opponent has defected
in either of the last two rounds. If so, it then checks if the opponent's score is higher than its own and defects if it is. In all
other cases (if the opponent has been cooperative), it will cooperate.

**22. Gradual:**
This strategy is forgiving at first but its punishments escalate over time. It starts by cooperating. When the opponent defects for
the Nth time, "Gradual" retaliates by defecting for N rounds, after which it returns to cooperating. Each subsequent defection from
the opponent results in a longer punishment phase.

**23. Random:**
As its name implies, this strategy makes its decision by flipping a coin. In every round, it has a 50% chance of cooperating and a
50% chance of defecting. Its behavior is completely unpredictable and is not influenced by the opponent's actions or the history of
the game.
